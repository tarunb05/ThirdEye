## 1. How to read this report

Faculty gave two levels of criteria: **mandatory** research-conference
requirements, and **additional** requirements for institutional reimbursement.
A third set of requirements was added subsequently — publication partner,
organising body, previously published proceedings, and the rule that
**everything must be verifiable on the official website, or confirmed by email
to the organisers.**

This report is organised so the final column of §3 answers the only question
that matters for a decision: *can we submit here, and what is still unknown?*

**Status vocabulary.**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Stated explicitly on the conference's own site, quoted in §4 |
| **Inferred** | Established from the publisher's own record of a previous edition, not from the conference site |
| **Not on official site** | We could not find it on the official site — **requires an email to the organisers** |

> **The EASE question is the important one.** ACM's ICPS open-access model can
> place the cost on the authors rather than on readers. A venue that looks
> cheapest on registration can be the most expensive overall. We do not know the
> figure and have not assumed one.

## 6. Recommendation

**Two submissions, two different artefacts, no overlapping content.**

**A. MSR 2027 Data & Tool Showcase — abstract 5 November, paper 10 November.**
Four pages describing the benchmark itself: 2,250 contracts, 1,125 safe : 1,125
vulnerable, with the safe class stratified by *how each safe label was obtained*
— audited library, audit-reviewed clean, real-world with no reported bug. To our
knowledge no existing Solidity benchmark permits a false-alarm rate to be
decomposed by label trustworthiness. The artefact exists, is checkpointed
per-contract, and needs no new experiments. This is achievable in the time
remaining precisely because the work is already done.

**B. EASE 2027 — abstract 15 January, paper 22 January.**
The full research paper. EASE is *Evaluation and Assessment in Software
Engineering*; our central claim is that a reported false-alarm rate is produced
by the evaluation apparatus as much as by the tool. The venue's subject and the
paper's subject are the same subject. Three months of writing time, publisher
openly stated, 31st edition.

**If in-country attendance is the deciding factor,** substitute ISEC's 8
November workshop track for (A). It is in Mumbai, the publication partner and
ACM DL archival are verified on the official site, and it is by far the
strongest reimbursement case. We did not lead with it because a workshop paper
carries less weight than a research-track paper — but that is a judgement about
prestige, and faculty may reasonably weigh cost and attendance higher.

## 8. Note on what this report does not claim

We have **not** verified that any of these venues is currently Scopus- or
Web-of-Science-indexed. Faculty's own brief makes the point: a conference
claiming to be Scopus-indexed does not make it so, and Scopus Sources frequently
lists a *proceedings series* rather than the individual conference. All five
publish through ACM or IEEE, which makes coverage likely — but likely is not
verified, and we would rather bring five confirmed rows than eight confident
ones.

We have also not quoted a registration fee for any venue, because none of them
has published one for 2027.
